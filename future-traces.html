05 — Sculpture & digital light study · 2025

Future Traces

Future Traces extends my hand-sculpted terracotta icon into a digital space.

Medium
Terracotta clay, glaze, volumetric render
Category
Phenomenological lighting study
Year
Autumn 2025

I made a 3D model and animation of my hand-sculpted terracotta icon standing upright and radiating light. It let me explore a vision that was difficult to realise with the physical sculpture.

I explored the symbolism of icons, hoping to make an object that carries emotion and leaves traces for future generations.

After my first clay prototype broke during firing, I developed a larger form using clay wrapped around a fabric-covered wooden structure.

The clay icon left this imprint on the cloth wrapped around its wooden support.

Once leather-hard, the clay could hold its shape independently.

Glazing required months of testing, yet the colours developed differently than expected.

Glaze tests and colour samples.

First, second and third firing.

The broken icon and a close view of its coloured surface and exposed clay.

I added roses, lilies and passion flowers. These flowers carry symbolism connected to Mary.

I sculpted the faces intuitively, allowing forms to emerge through touch rather than controlling every detail, as if acting as a transmitter rather than the sole author.

A crack appeared naturally through Mary after two firings, right where I had imagined it. It spoke to my worry that spirituality might one day shatter, but I could never bring myself to create it.

The finished icon, with the natural crack running across Mary's face.

When it appeared naturally, it felt like a gift from the material.

The sculpture gradually developed a character of its own, moving beyond my initial intentions. I began to imagine these details as traces left for future generations, inviting them to imagine the meaning behind them.

The sculpture came from my grief and concern about the direction of the world. I value what technology makes possible, but worry about what we may lose as we become disconnected from our spiritual nature.

I wanted the expressions and details of the work to speak for me.

Mary occupies the centre of the composition. Her painted surface and blue colour convey melancholy, while her brokenness becomes an image of a wounded spirit.

On the back, I engraved a message in Georgian:

Helia, this is me.
Do not forget the spirit ours.

The inscription identifies me as the maker while leaving a message for whoever might discover the work in the future.

I wanted to make an object that carries the concerns of its time into an uncertain future—something a new civilisation might discover long after its original context has disappeared.

In the animation, the icon stands upright and radiates light. I wanted to see how light could change the sculpture’s presence and express its spiritual meaning.
