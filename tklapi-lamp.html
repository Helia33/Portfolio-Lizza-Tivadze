02 — Material-led lighting · 2025

Tklapi Lamp

A Georgian sun-dried fruit leather becomes a lampshade that glows like a firework catching light.

Material
Sun-dried fruit leather (tklapi), copper framework
Object
Translucent lamp
Year
Autumn 2025

I use tklapi, a traditional Georgian sun-dried fruit leather, to make a textured and translucent lampshade. I wanted to see how this familiar food could become a light object while keeping its connection to Georgian culture.

From fruit to sheet

I crush the fruit into a purée and spread it in a thin layer. Slow sun-drying removes the moisture and leaves a flexible sheet that lets light pass through.

The fruit leather before and after the light passes through it.

Testing how the panels fit the frame and respond to light.

Letting the material lead

I coiled copper wire into a form inspired by a caterpillar. The finished lamp no longer resembles one exactly. I let the copper and tklapi guide the shape as I worked.

Hand-stitching joins the panels and gives the shade its structure.

I enjoyed allowing the materials to influence the outcome rather than forcing the form to match my initial idea.

Technical drawings documenting the lamp’s frame, shade construction and measurements.

Resolution

The dried tklapi stayed stable and was pleasant to work with. The project made me curious about how it could be used beyond food.

Tklapi Lamp: the finished object, illuminated.

Additional views of the lamp from different sides, with close details of the glowing panels, seams and socket.
