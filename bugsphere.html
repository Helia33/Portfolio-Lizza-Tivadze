06 — Immersive film · Spring 2026

Bugsphere

Follow the ladybug and the world turns bright. Look away, and the water begins to rise.

Format
Animation
Medium
3D animation, live footage, sound
Length
47 seconds
Year
Spring 2026

Bugsphere is a short film about the choice between noticing hope and sinking into everything that weighs on us. A ladybug stands for the positive thinking of the people of Terneuzen. Following it opens a bright world. Turning away from it leads to the sea.

Bugsphere, 47 seconds, with sound. Best watched full screen.

Where it began

While interviewing people in Terneuzen, I heard many things they did not like about the place. Alongside them I also heard small signs of hope that were easy to miss. The ladybug became a way to point at those signs, so that people might notice them.

The street

The animation begins in the familiar city. The street is slightly out of focus, as if seen by someone who is not really looking, and everything feels grey and ordinary.

Terneuzen at the start of the animation: a street we pass without noticing.

Hope is a direction.

The ladybug appears

Then a small red shape appears in a roadside shrub. It hides among the leaves and opens its wings, inviting the viewer to follow.

The ladybug in the shrub, hiding among the leaves, then opening its wings.

Following the ladybug

Following it carries the viewer into a bright meadow of poppies, flowers and grass. The camera moves through branches and petals until the ladybug is found resting on a white flower.

Passing through the branches into the meadow.

The ladybug, found at last on a flower in the grass.

Looking away

Losing sight of the ladybug carries the viewer into a dark and heavy sea. Both worlds are built from the same place, and what changes is where you choose to look. Rain gathers, and the waves begin to rise.

Rain gathers, and the waves begin to rise.

The end

In the original version the film ended on a black screen, as a way to say that the person had drowned. I kept that silence, but let the sound of the waves carry on and fade slowly while the last words appear.

The sea moves from a distant horizon into darkness.

Making the film

I worked from my original animation and re-edited it into a square fisheye, close to the feel of 360 footage. I slowed the key moments down, added short captions in Nyala, graded each world separately and kept the sound of the ladybug’s wings and the sea.

Looking closer

These photographs come from encounters with ladybugs in Terneuzen. I found them on leaves, among wild plants and even on the pavement. Each small red shell invited me to pause and look more closely at the life around me.

A ladybug among dandelions, in the grass, and crossing the pavement.

Different places, the same bright visitor.

Looking closer reveals more than one ladybug.

Three ladybugs gathered on a leaf.

Finding the spheres

The ceramic spheres are an allegory of the ladybugs I encountered outside. Their rounded forms recall the small insects, while their pale surfaces make them feel like quiet discoveries in the landscape. Finding a sphere tucked among plants echoes the moment of spotting a ladybug: you have to look closely, and the familiar world suddenly feels full of life and possibility. Opening one reveals a colorful, playful world inside.

The ceramic spheres and other small vessels on display.

From studies to setting

The photographs move from workshop studies to the spheres placed outdoors. Set among leaves, soil and other everyday details, each one becomes something to discover—just like finding a ladybug in the real world.

From the first sphere studies to a quiet discovery among the garden plants.

The spheres among the planting, waiting to be noticed.
